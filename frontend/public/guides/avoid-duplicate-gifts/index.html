How to avoid duplicate gifts without ruining the surprise

Duplicate gifts happen when several people shop for the same person and nobody can see what the others have bought. The fix is shared information, but sharing it the wrong way can spoil the surprise. Here is what works.

Why standard wishlists fall short

Many store wishlists show buyers what has been purchased, but they also show the owner. If you can see that an item is gone, the surprise is gone with it. Other lists don't track purchases at all, so duplicates slip through.

What actually works

  • One list, one link. Put every wish in a single place and give the same link to everyone who might buy something.
  • Let buyers mark what they bought. The moment someone claims an item, everyone else sees it and picks something different.
  • Hide purchases from the recipient. The person the gifts are for should see their own wishes, not the claims.
  • Decide on a cutoff. Ask people to mark items as soon as they buy, not on the way to the party.
  • Add a range of prices. A mix of small and big items means people on any budget can find something.

Using BlindList for this

BlindList creates two links for each list: one for you to manage it, and one for the people buying. Buyers see what has been claimed; you don't. If you're not sure which link to send, it is the buyer link.

A short message to send with the link

“Here's my list for my birthday. Please mark whatever you buy so nobody doubles up. I can't see what's been marked, so it'll still be a surprise.”
