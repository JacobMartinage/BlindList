BlindList

A kid's wishlist for grandparents, aunts and uncles

Relatives want to give something the child will love, and parents want to avoid a pile of duplicates and things that won't fit in the house. A shared list solves both.

How to set it up

  1. Make the list yourself. Parents choose what goes on it, so the child can't be surprised by something unsuitable.
  2. Add a range. A few small gifts, a few mid-range, one or two big ones, and some that are not toys, such as clothes, books or lessons.
  3. Include sizes and details. Age ranges, sizes and the exact model prevent returns.
  4. Send the buyer link to relatives. Ask them to mark what they buy.
  5. Keep the manager link private. It lets you change the list.

Etiquette that helps

  • Say that the list is a guide, not a rule, so relatives who prefer to pick something themselves feel welcome.
  • Mention if you want to limit toys, or prefer experiences or savings.
  • Update the list as the child's interests change.

Why a blind list suits kids

If the child, or the parent managing the list, sees what has been claimed, the surprise on the day is gone. With BlindList, relatives see what has been marked as bought; the person managing the list does not.
